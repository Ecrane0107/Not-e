Why the number isn't the same for everyone

Your body spends a baseline amount of energy just staying alive — your basal metabolic rate. It moves with four things:

  • Weight — more mass costs more energy to run.
  • Height — a taller frame carries more tissue at the same weight.
  • Age — the baseline drifts down by roughly 5 kcal a year.
  • Sex — on average men carry more muscle, which puts the baseline about 150–200 kcal higher at the same height, weight and age.

Then activity multiplies it. A desk job with no training lands near 1.2×; training hard six days a week is closer to 1.7×. That total is your maintenance — eat roughly that and weight holds steady.

From there, a deficit of about 500 kcal a day tends to cost in the region of a pound a week, and a surplus of 200–400 is a common starting point for gaining. Which is why a goal that's right for one person can be hundreds of calories off for another.
